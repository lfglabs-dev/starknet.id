Managing long, error-prone hexadecimal addresses can be a hassle. A unified Starknet domain system offers a simpler way. This guide compares both approaches to help you decide on the best identity solution for your activity on Starknet.

If you're active on Starknet, you're likely used to copying, pasting, and double-checking 64-character hexadecimal strings for every transaction. A single mistyped character can derail the whole process. Here, we’ll compare these traditional addresses against Starknet domains, looking at key practical differences and walking you through how to adopt either system.

## Step 1: Evaluate Address Management Requirements

### Security and Error Prevention
**Hexadecimal addresses**: You have to manually verify every 64-character string. A single typo can lead to a failed transaction or even lost funds.

**Starknet domains**: Their human-readable names make typos much less likely. Plus, built-in validation helps catch common mistakes before they become a problem.

### Integration Complexity
**Hexadecimal addresses**: These work out-of-the-box with the blockchain. There’s no extra setup, but they are limited to basic sending and receiving.

**Starknet domains**: You need to mint an NFT to get started. Once you do, you can use a single name across many dApps and even use it to store and share data.

## Step 2: Compare Operational Workflows

### Transaction Process
**Hexadecimal workflow**:
1. Copy the full 64-character address.
2. Verify each character segment.
3. Paste it into the transaction field.
4. Double-check before confirming.

**Domain workflow**:
1. Enter your memorable .stark domain.
2. The system automatically finds the correct address.
3. Confirm the transaction.

### Multi-dApp Management
**Hexadecimal addresses**: You have to keep track of different addresses for each application, with no way to link your identity across platforms.

**Starknet domains**: Your single domain name works on any integrated dApp. In some cases, your data can even sync automatically.

## Step 3: Assess Technical Capabilities

### Account Abstraction Support
Starknet has native account abstraction, which allows for advanced identity features you won't find on Ethereum. Starknet ID is built on this architecture to provide more functionality. With a standard hexadecimal address, you're limited to basic functions. In contrast, a domain takes full advantage of account abstraction, opening the door to features like programmable identity logic.

### Developer Integration
For developers, integrating domains is becoming easier. Starknet.js v3 now supports human-readable names, making integration seamless. What's more, the Starknet MCP Server even allows AI agents to interact with the system using natural language.

## Step 4: Making Your Decision

**Stick with hexadecimal addresses if:**
- You perform fewer than five transactions per month.
- You only use basic send/receive functions.
- You prefer to have zero setup.

**Go with a Starknet domain if:**
- You interact with multiple Starknet dApps.
- You want to reduce errors and improve your experience.
- You want to manage your on-chain identity from one place.

## Step 5: Getting Started

### Setting Up Your Starknet Domain:
1. Navigate to starknet.id.
2. Connect your Starknet wallet.
3. Mint your Starknet ID NFT.
4. Link your preferred .stark domain.
5. Configure your identity data as needed.

### Sticking with Hexadecimal Addresses:
1. Keep your addresses stored in a secure system.
2. Create a process for verifying addresses character-by-character.
3. Make use of address book features in wallets and dApps when you can.

## Our Recommendation: Go for a Starknet Domain

The practical benefits of a human-readable name and a single identity for all your activities easily outweigh the small effort of setting one up. Because Starknet ID is based on NFTs, it's a flexible solution that will grow with the ecosystem.
